# Two-paragraph breakdown

*Kept short and always up to date — see `.agent/STATUS.md` for full detail, `.agent/ARS.md` for every finding, `.agent/DECISIONS.md` for the reasoning behind real decisions.*

This is a full FreeRTOS rewrite of a coffee-grinder scale's ESP32 firmware (branch `rewrite/rtos-fork`, effectively a permanent fork of `main`): seven tasks (scale sampling, dosing control, input, display, settings, network, telemetry) communicating over queues/mailboxes instead of the old single `loop()`/`switch(state)` machine, a fitted online dosing model (`lib/DosingModel`) replacing a static lookup table, a React/TypeScript SPA served from the device's own LittleFS filesystem at `http://eureka.local/`, and telemetry posted directly to a PostgREST deployment the SPA's History tab also queries straight from the browser. The rewrite has been running on the real physical device through several live-debugging sessions: a stack of real hardware/firmware bugs were found and fixed this way (buttons wired active-high/active-low asymmetrically but assumed uniform, the load cell's excitation never powered, ADC gain/speed clobbered by init ordering, an OTA display race, a grind-start-baseline bug and a separate mid-grind sensor-glitch bug that both let a session falsely "finish" early, and a settings-exposure gap that made most ADC/timing tunables unreachable from the SPA). An attempt at dynamically switching the ADC's sample rate (fast while changing, precise once settled) was built three ways, oscillated live each time for the same underlying reason (the ADS1232 driver's own "changing" flag isn't a reliable real-time trigger at any single sample rate), and was reverted at the owner's request — logged as a standing finding (AR-050) rather than re-attempted blindly later.

Most recently, the owner used the device for real dosing for the first time since a display/SPA visual redesign (both surfaces now use real iOS/macOS system colors and, on the SPA, the `-apple-system` font stack) and reported three issues, all fixed: topup pulses never fired at all — traced to a genuine structural bug where the cold-start noise estimate, at the safety margin the code used, exactly consumed the entire allowed overshoot budget with zero room left for any pulse, made permanent by `TopupModelV1` never persisting across reboots (both now fixed, the margin choice made with the owner's explicit sign-off); the FINALIZE screen's timer kept counting instead of freezing; and auto-tare fired almost immediately after a dose instead of giving a grace period to see the result. A pass over the rest of the codebase also turned up that the idle-screensaver feature was fully implemented but literally unreachable (no idle timer ever triggered it) — now fixed, and per the owner's earlier decision it blanks the panel (backlight off) rather than showing a clock, addressing a real TFT burn-in concern (a follow-up tying panel power to the coffee machine's own on/off state via Home Assistant is intentionally not started yet; it now also wakes on a large weight change, not just a button press). Because diagnosing the topup bug required actually explaining the dosing algorithm, that became a real feature too: a new SPA "Model" tab shows the fitted grind-rate/coast/topup parameters live, next to the actual formulas that use them, since the owner wanted to understand what the algorithm does rather than take it on faith. Reviewing that explanation, the owner caught two more real issues from direct 7-year hardware experience: the rate/coast models decayed with a 45-day recency half-life for no reason (this grinder hasn't drifted in 7 years — now disabled, matching the topup model's existing no-decay default), and the topup pulse duration had no hard floor tied to the physical relay's actual minimum actuation time (~300ms, below which it just stalls and produces zero output — now a real 350ms floor independent of whatever the model has learned).
