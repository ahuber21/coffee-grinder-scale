# Two-paragraph breakdown

*Kept short and always up to date — see `.agent/STATUS.md` for full detail, `.agent/ARS.md` for every finding, `.agent/DECISIONS.md` for the reasoning behind real decisions.*

This is a full FreeRTOS rewrite of a coffee-grinder scale's ESP32 firmware (branch `rewrite/rtos-fork`, effectively a permanent fork of `main`): seven tasks (scale sampling, dosing control, input, display, settings, network, telemetry) communicating over queues/mailboxes instead of the old single `loop()`/`switch(state)` machine, a fitted online dosing model (`lib/DosingModel`) replacing a static lookup table, a React/TypeScript SPA served from the device's own LittleFS filesystem at `http://eureka.local/`, and telemetry posted directly to a PostgREST deployment the SPA's History tab also queries straight from the browser. The rewrite has been running on the real physical device through several live-debugging sessions: a stack of real hardware/firmware bugs were found and fixed this way (buttons wired active-high/active-low asymmetrically but assumed uniform, the load cell's excitation never powered, ADC gain/speed clobbered by init ordering, an OTA display race, a grind-start-baseline bug and a separate mid-grind sensor-glitch bug that both let a session falsely "finish" early, and a settings-exposure gap that made most ADC/timing tunables unreachable from the SPA). An attempt at dynamically switching the ADC's sample rate (fast while changing, precise once settled) was built three ways, oscillated live each time for the same underlying reason (the ADS1232 driver's own "changing" flag isn't a reliable real-time trigger at any single sample rate), and was reverted at the owner's request — logged as a standing finding (AR-050) rather than re-attempted blindly later.

The owner then used the device for real dosing for the first time since a display/SPA visual redesign (both surfaces now use real iOS/macOS system colors) and reported issues that led to a real architecture reversal in the topup logic. First: topup pulses never fired at all, zero-margin structural bug in the fitted-line topup model plus `TopupModelV1` never persisting across reboots (both fixed); FINALIZE's timer kept running instead of freezing (fixed); auto-tare fired immediately after a dose instead of after a grace period (fixed, 10s); and the idle-screensaver feature turned out to be fully built but literally unreachable — no idle timer ever triggered it (fixed; per the owner's earlier decision it blanks the panel rather than showing a clock, and now also wakes on a large weight change, not just a button press). Diagnosing the topup bug required explaining the dosing algorithm, so that became a real feature: a new SPA "Model" tab shows the fitted parameters live next to the formulas that use them. Reviewing that explanation, the owner — with 7 years of hands-on experience with this exact grinder — caught that the models were applying a 45-day recency decay for no reason (now disabled) and that topup pulse durations had no hard floor tied to the relay's real ~300ms stall threshold (now a 350ms floor). Continued live testing then exposed the deeper issue: the topup model's fitted-line assumption (pulse output scales smoothly with duration) doesn't hold at short durations, which behave as a discrete, clumpy release process — real measured noise (0.23g) was large enough to hit the same zero-margin deadlock again, and the owner's own diagnosis ("nothing intentional about this behavior... just randomly adding clumps") was exactly right. Replaced entirely with a self-tuning 10-bucket (0.1g granularity) lookup table of pulse durations — recovered the pre-rewrite firmware's own hand-tuned bucket scheme from git history, but now updated from real pulses via an asymmetric online rule instead of by hand — a deliberate reversal of the earlier "replace the lookup table with a fitted model" decision (D7), documented as D23. The required schema bump also reset the main-grind/coast models' accumulated confidence (not just the topup fields), disclosed as a known, low-impact side effect since their point estimates had already converged to the compiled-in priors. A live 18g grind test confirmed the redesigned LUT behaves as intended (overshoot corrected faster than undershoot grows, as designed), after which the owner requested two small follow-ups: the ADS1232 ring buffer's max size raised from 48 to 96 samples (the `speed`/SPS setting itself is unchanged — still static, defaulting to 10, per AR-050's earlier revert), and a historical time-series chart of the topup LUT's 10 bucket durations added to the Model tab so a bucket settling in over real pulses is visible at a glance.
