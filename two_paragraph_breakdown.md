# Two-paragraph breakdown

*Kept short; see `.agent/STATUS.md` for current detail, `.agent/ARS.md` for every finding and `.agent/DECISIONS.md` for the reasoning behind decisions.*

This is the firmware for an ESP32 scale built into a coffee grinder. It weighs the grounds as they fall, stops the grinder at a target dose and tops up with short relay pulses to close the remaining gap. Seven FreeRTOS tasks (scale sampling, dosing control, input, display, settings, network, telemetry) communicate over queues and mailboxes. Dosing aims short of the target by a margin, using an online model of the grind rate and of the "coast" that lands after the relay switches off, and a landing learner that adjusts the stop point from the settled weight of past grinds. A self-tuning lookup table of pulse durations, one per remaining-gap size, then closes the gap. A React and TypeScript web app is served from the device's own filesystem at `http://eureka.local/`; it shows the live dose, edits every setting, and queries a PostgREST deployment directly for session history and accuracy charts, which the firmware fills in as it doses.

Settings are described once, in `lib/Messaging/SettingsSchema.h`, which drives their persistence in NVS, live writes and the WebSocket protocol. The pure-logic modules (the models and the settings schema) are unit-tested on the host, and the display code also compiles on the host, where a simulator renders scripted scenarios and checks them against golden frame hashes. The goal now is accuracy: first make the main grind settle on its offset target, and once that is proven, dose the real target directly and make topup optional.
